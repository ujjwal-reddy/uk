Act I — The Global Problem

An atmosphere carrying more than it can hold.

Every year the planet absorbs carbon faster than it can lock it away. Heavy industry, deep borewell extraction, fossil-fuel combustion, synthetic nitrogen fertilizers and deforestation each push a stream of CO₂ into a shell that was never designed to store it — while the soils that should be sinking that carbon are stripped of the organic matter that lets them hold it.

  • Industrial & thermal combustionContinuous point-source CO₂, SOx and particulate loading over semi-arid districts.
  • Deep borewell extractionFalling water tables force deeper bores, higher energy draw and rising salt concentration in irrigation water.
  • Chemical fertilizer dependenceUrea-heavy regimes acidify the root zone, collapse microbial life and vent N₂O — ~273× the warming potential of CO₂.
  • Deforestation & canopy lossRemoval of standing biomass deletes the fastest natural sink and exposes topsoil to wind and heat erosion.
  • Open residue burningPost-harvest stubble and prunings burned in the open release stored carbon in hours instead of banking it for centuries.

Regional case study

Anantapur & Sri Sathya Sai, Andhra Pradesh

CE² was founded inside the crisis it is built to solve. These two districts sit in India's second-driest rain shadow — an agricultural economy running on a rainfall budget that no longer arrives, on soils that are simultaneously going saline and going acidic.

522mmAverage annual rainfall — a fraction of what the cropping pattern assumes.
2×stressSoil degradation on both fronts: salinity from borewell irrigation, acidification from chemical inputs.
365daysNear-continuous agricultural residue burning — groundnut shells, cotton stems, orchard prunings.
∞spreadInvasive Prosopis juliflora colonising fallow and grazing land, drawing down what little groundwater remains.

The compounding loop: less rain → deeper borewells → saltier irrigation → weaker soil structure → more chemical input → more acidification → lower yield → more residue burned → more atmospheric carbon → less rain.
