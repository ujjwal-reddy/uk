Act I · The smoke

Every plume here is carbon leaving the ground for good.

Carbon dioxide is not an abstraction in this district — it is smoke you can see. Factory stacks, brick and charcoal kilns, diesel borewell pumps, field fires at the end of every harvest and the cooking fire in every kitchen. Each one takes carbon that was locked in biomass or rock and puts it into the air in hours.

The land

An agricultural economy running on a rainfall budget that no longer arrives.

CE² was founded inside the crisis it is built to solve. These two districts sit in a deep rain shadow, on soils going saline and acidic at the same time.

The compounding loop

less rain → deeper borewells → saltier irrigation → weaker soil structure → more chemical input → more acidification → lower yield → more residue burned → more atmospheric carbon → less rain.

Act IV · The cycle

How the carbon actually gets restored.

Carbon restoration is a loop, not a line. Air to plant, plant to kiln, kiln to soil — and from the soil the next crop grows and the loop turns again, leaving a little more carbon in the ground each time round.

Tap any station on the ring for what happens there.

Why the soil holds it

Pyrolysis rearranges the carbon in biomass into fused aromatic rings. Soil microbes that would strip fresh organic matter in a season cannot break that structure down — so the carbon sits in the pore network for centuries instead of returning to the atmosphere.

DurableCentury-to-millennium residence time in soil, not a decadal one.
DocumentedEvery batch and every field application is logged.
HonestOnly carbon that goes into the soil is counted as stored — fuel is fuel.
